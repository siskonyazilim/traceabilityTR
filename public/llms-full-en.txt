### Solutions & Products

**Individual Product Tracking (Track & Trace)**
Ensures that each product has a unique number (UID) and tracks this number through every step of the process. One of the key criteria is the product material type. Available marking solutions: inkjet, thermal transfer, laser, carbon fiber.

**Lot/Batch Tracking**
Preferred where tracking individual products is difficult. The batch is defined by the manufacturing date and lot number, and follows through packaging, warehousing and delivery to the end consumer. Both methodologies can be applied simultaneously.

**Pick to Light (P2L)**
P2L solutions provide operators with visual guidance for manual production and assembly processes. Correct or incorrect situations are shown with lights or optional audio warnings. Completed work can be automatically approved and integrated with other systems.

**RTLS — Real Time Location Systems**
Systems for automatically identifying and tracking the location of objects or people in real time. Wireless RTLS tags can be attached to objects or worn by people. Fixed reference nodes allow precise position determination.

**WMS — Warehouse Management Systems**
A system that allows organizations to control and manage the entry and exit of goods or materials in the warehouse. Operates with real-time data, providing the most up-to-date information about all activities conducted within the warehouse.

**ERP/MES Integration**
The necessary combination of applications and solutions to enable data transfer between independent applications. Integration with SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics via RESTful APIs, SOAP web services or custom connectors. Standard industrial protocols: OPC-UA, TMC.

## FAQ — Our Perspectives on Traceability

**What is industrial Track & Trace traceability?**
Track & Trace traceability is a critical system that enables individual product tracking throughout the entire lifecycle, from package level to pallet level. The system ensures that each product receives a unique identifier (UID) that is digitally recorded at all stages of production, packaging, storage and distribution. Modern traceability systems collect and analyze data in real time, providing complete visibility and compliance with strict industrial regulations.

**What is Aggregation and why is it critical for traceability?**
Aggregation is the most vital element of modern traceability. It represents the process of creating a digital hierarchy in which the unique identifiers (UIDs) of individual products are associated with the identifier of the larger package containing them, forming a parent-child relationship. Standard hierarchy: Package → Carton → Case → Pallet. Any association error (Aggregation Mismatch) causes immediate machine stoppage and product rejection.

**What is the difference between Serialization (UID) and Lot Tracking?**
Serialization means assigning a completely unique identifier to each individual product. A UID typically contains: manufacturer ID, GTIN/SKU, unique serial number, aggregation reference and a checksum or cryptographic signature. GS1 SGTIN (for serialized products) and GS1 SSCC (for logistics units) standards are widely used. Lot tracking groups products manufactured in the same period under a common lot identifier based on production date and lot number. Both methodologies can work simultaneously.

**How do industrial camera verification systems work?**
High-resolution industrial cameras placed on the production line automatically scan each code in real time after application, verifying readability, contrast, dimensions and data correctness. If the camera cannot read the code (No Read), the product is immediately marked for rejection and physically removed via an automatic reject mechanism. Modern cameras can process over 2,000 products per minute, detecting defects invisible to the human eye.

**What coding technologies are used in traceability?**
Three main technologies: (1) Laser coding — preferred for high-speed lines (>2,000 products/minute), permanent marking without consumable costs; (2) Inkjet coding — used mainly at logistics level for cases and pallets, excellent contrast; (3) Direct Data Matrix 2D marking — industrial standard for serialization, stores large amounts of data in small space, can be read even if partially damaged.

**How is integration with corporate ERP and MES systems achieved?**
Standard data flow: UID Generator → Printers → Industrial cameras → Central Aggregation Server → MES/MII layers via OPC-UA and TMC → ERP systems (SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics) via RESTful APIs, SOAP web services or custom connectors. End-to-end integration completely eliminates manual data entry.

**How is compliance with regulations and GS1 standards ensured?**
Systems automatically generate GS1-compliant identifiers (SGTIN for individual products, SSCC for logistics units), including checksums and cryptographic signatures for anti-counterfeiting. Covers ISO 9001, IATF 16949 (automotive), ISO 22000/HACCP (food), TTT and EU TPD (tobacco). Audit reports are generated automatically, reducing preparation time by over 70%.

**How long does it take to implement a complete traceability system?**
A typical project takes 4–8 months: process analysis, architecture design, hardware installation, server configuration, OPC-UA integration development, MES/ERP connectivity setup, extensive testing, personnel training, post-implementation support. Large projects (multiple lines, complex compliance requirements) may take 10–14 months. Modular approach allows progressive commissioning.

### The Importance of Food Traceability for End Consumers
**URL:** https://traceability.ro/en/blog/the-importance-of-food-traceability-for-end-consumers
**Date:** November 25, 2024

Food traceability plays an essential role in ensuring food safety, quality and consumer satisfaction. FAO defines food traceability as "the recording and tracking of food product production, processing and distribution processes."

Key benefits for end consumers:
- **Food safety:** Traceability systems help quickly identify and resolve risks, facilitating efficient product recalls. During the 2006 E. coli outbreak, lack of traceability damaged many brands' reputations (FDA, 2007).
- **Transparency and trust:** 66% of consumers look for information about sustainable and ethical production (Nielsen, 2015).
- **Quality perception:** 60% of consumers are willing to pay more for higher quality products (Mintel, 2020).
- **Social responsibility:** 73% of consumers are inclined to buy sustainable products (McKinsey, 2020).
- **Differentiation and preference:** High traceability helps brands stand out in competitive markets.

Predictions for the next five years: digitalization and blockchain adoption, growing consumer demands (80% will require more food safety information — FMI, 2021), stricter regulations, sustainability focus.

**Date:** July 23, 2024

### How to Implement Individual Product Traceability?
**URL:** https://traceability.ro/en/blog/how-to-implement-individual-product-traceability
**Date:** October 5, 2021

The first essential rule of individual traceability is assigning a unique code to each product. Critical questions to analyze:
1. Is the product structure suitable for marking? Where should the marking be applied? (Involves marketing, sales, quality, R&D and production departments)
2. What operations require advance marking? (Modular approach — start with critical operations)
3. Can the applied code remain readable throughout all production operations? (Ovens, painting, sandblasting can affect readability)
4. What marking technology should be used? (Inkjet, thermal transfer, laser/carbon fiber, RFID — based on the previous analysis)

Cycle time, process flow, machine positioning and space constraints can all influence these answers. The second critical step: ensuring each code is scanned before each operation.

### What Are the Benefits of Traceability Systems?
**URL:** https://traceability.ro/en/blog/what-are-the-benefits-of-traceability-systems
**Date:** October 4, 2021

In a competitive manufacturing environment, differentiation comes through quality — production with low scrap and rework rates. Benefits:
- **Improved production quality** — integrated control points prevent critical errors.
- **Reduced rework and production costs** — early error detection resolves issues quickly.
- **Root Cause Analysis support** — access to data from every stage of production.
- **Clarification of cost analysis between operations** — clear visibility of cost and value flows.
- **Facilitation of continuous improvement** — monitoring part flow between lines enables real-time optimization.

In certain industries (healthcare, pharmaceutical) regulations mandate traceability systems. This trend is expanding to more industries.
